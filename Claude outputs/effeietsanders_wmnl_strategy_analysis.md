# WMNL 2027–2030: how it fits together, what not to miss, what's missing, and where you'll likely push back

*Based on the Meerjarenstrategie 2027-2030 PDF, the 2022-2024 English strategy PDF for comparison, and the nl.wikimedia.org draft-strategy talk pages you commented on directly in July 2026.*

## 1. How the document is actually structured

**Two ambitions, asymmetric in scope.** Ambition 1 (invest in a strong community and durable partnerships) is inward-facing and covers three of the five goals — Doelstelling 1 (volunteer support), 2 (new editors), 3 (technology). Ambition 2 (advocate for the open ecosystem and reliable information) is outward-facing and covers only two — Doelstelling 4 (informed citizens) and 5 (open domain priority with policymakers). It's a 3–2 split, not the even split the "twee ambities" framing might suggest.

**The positioning statement is rhetorically central but operationally narrow.** "Kennis is menselijk" is introduced before the ambitions, as if it frames the whole plan. Mechanically it only feeds Doelstelling 4.1 ("Communiceren positionering") — one activity line out of eleven across the document. The technology working group, moderator support, onboarding, and coalition-building all proceed without visible reference back to it. Either that's fine — a positioning line doesn't need its own workstream — or it signals the plan hasn't worked out how "Kennis is menselijk" should actually shape Doelstelling 1-3, which is where the AI-adjacent work happens.

**Each doelstelling follows the same shape**: problem statement → 1-2 activiteitenprogramma's → succesindicatoren → one kernindicator with a target year and named measurement method. Consistent discipline, but the rigor behind "succesindicatoren" varies a lot (§2).

**Randvoorwaarden (organisatie/bestuur, financiën) sit outside the five doelstellingen** as enabling conditions, not goals with their own metrics. Reasonable structurally, but it also means the plan's only acknowledgment that current staff/board capacity might not match its ambitions is tucked into a bullet under "Prioriteiten organisatie" rather than treated as a risk to the five goals themselves (§2).

**Lost versus 2022-2024: explicit traceability to Movement Strategy 2030.** The 2022 plan named which numbered MS2030 recommendation each priority answered to (1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8). The 2027 plan doesn't carry this forward — "why these five goals, specifically, and how do they connect to the global movement's own strategy" is harder to audit from the document alone than last cycle.

## 2. What not to miss

- **Doelstelling 3.2 (the new technology working group) is the plan's one genuinely new governance structure.** Stated as a single bullet — "we richten een samenwerkingsverband op, volgens het model van het coördinatorenoverleg" — but it's a real commitment: a new standing group, staff time, ongoing coordination overhead. It reads as low-key relative to what it actually is.
- **The board has already flagged that current staff can't fully deliver this plan as scoped.** Under "Prioriteiten organisatie," bolded in the source: "Samenstelling staf in lijn brengen met de ambities zoals gedefinieerd in de meerjarenstrategie." That's an admitted staffing gap against the plan's own ambitions, not a footnote.
- **The 2030 financial figures are explicitly marked provisional** — "Bedragen nog definitief vast te stellen." Read the whole Financiën chapter, including the €655K 2030 projection, as a placeholder scenario, not a locked number.
- **The colofon's ratification date is blank** — "Vastgesteld tijdens ALV (datum nog invullen)." Worth confirming whether this is the version actually approved by the membership, or a late pre-ALV draft still circulating.
- **Success-indicator rigor is uneven across the five goals.** Doelstelling 1, 3, and 5 use structured surveys with numeric targets (≥7 by 2028/2029). Doelstelling 4's kernindicator is LinkedIn follower count (884 → 1,300 by 2030) standing in for "burgers zijn beter geïnformeerd" — a much weaker proxy than the other four use.
- **Doelstelling 2's core KPI (50 new editors/year) is measured "via de event-tool."** That ties the plan's headline recruitment metric to a specific WMF tool whose mandatory-use policy is the subject of active, unresolved dispute elsewhere in the movement — a dependency the plan doesn't name or hedge against.

## 3. What's missing from the document

1. **No diversity/representativeness baseline or KPI.** The 2022-2024 plan opened its inclusion chapter with concrete numbers — 11% female editors vs. 51% of the Dutch population, 3% vs. 13% non-Western migration background — as the central tracked weakness. The 2027-2030 plan folds diversity language into Doelstelling 2's activities without restating those numbers or setting a comparably concrete target. Either the gap has closed (unlikely on this timescale) or it's been dropped as a headline metric without comment — worth checking the SWOT_WMNL_2026 annex to see if it survived there instead of here.
2. **No mapping to Movement Strategy 2030's numbered recommendations**, unlike 2022-2024 (§1). Harder to audit the plan against the movement's own stated priorities.
3. **No stated position on whether Wikimedia content should be available for AI training, and on what terms.** This sits directly upstream of "Kennis is menselijk" — the plan asserts human-made content is the differentiator, but is silent on the access question that actually determines who benefits from that differentiator going forward. You raised exactly this on the draft talk page (citing the UVA Archival AI Protocol); it isn't resolved in the final text.
4. **No flexibility or contingency clause given AI uncertainty**, despite the plan naming that uncertainty explicitly as context. You raised this directly during drafting ("Algemene opmerking: flexibiliteit," 7 July 2026); it didn't make it into the approved text. The plan still sets specific 2028/2029/2030 targets against a backdrop it describes as fast-moving.
5. **No risk register or resource-contention discussion across the five goals.** Doelstelling 1's moderator support, Doelstelling 2's event-based recruiting, and Doelstelling 3's new technology working group all draw on the same small pool of technically able, already-active volunteers. The plan doesn't address what happens when these compete for the same people's time.
6. **The three referenced annexes (Pestel, SWOT_WMNL_2026, Confrontatiematrix_2026) aren't summarized anywhere in the plan itself.** A reader relying only on this document is missing the actual environmental scan that supposedly generated the five goals.
7. **No detail behind "align staff composition with ambitions."** Flagged as a board/staff priority (§2) with no indication of what it concretely means — new roles, reallocated roles, headcount growth, or a different mix within the same six-person staff.
8. **No new mechanism behind the (repeated) income-diversification goal.** Same prescription as 2022-2024 — grow donations, legacies, project grants — and 2025's numbers (WMF still ~74% of income) don't show that prescription working yet. No new lever is named that wasn't already tried last cycle.

## 4. Where you're likely to agree, and where you're likely to push back

This is grounded in what you said directly on the draft talk pages of this specific plan, plus tensions internal to the document itself — not general inference.

**Likely to sit well with you:**
- The mission/vision wording that was finalized matches the specific edits you and other commenters proposed on the talk page in June-July 2026.
- Accessibility ("toegankelijkheid") no longer appears as one of the three headlined core values — consistent with the concern you raised on the talk page that an absolute "always freely accessible" framing no longer reflects the tradeoffs the movement is actually facing.
- AI being named as a distinct strategic dimension at all (Doelstelling 3), even though not with the prominence you asked for (below).

**Likely to draw pushback from you:**
- **AI didn't land as the elevated, mission-adjacent "transformatiedoel" you proposed on the talk page** on 3 July 2026 ("Strategisch speerpunt? Transformatiedoel?"). It became Doelstelling 3 of 5 — one parallel goal among others, not a cross-cutting priority sitting beside mission and vision. Worth raising again now, while the plan is fresh, rather than after a year of execution has already assumed the current structure.
- **No flexibility commitment survived**, despite you asking for one directly on 7 July 2026 — the plan commits to fixed 2028-2030 targets against uncertainty it names but doesn't build a release valve for.
- **Doelstelling 3.2's new technology working group** is a fresh standing committee, modeled explicitly on an existing one (the coordinatorenoverleg) rather than folded into it. Worth deciding deliberately whether a parallel body is the right call here, or whether the same function belongs inside an existing structure.
- **Doelstelling 2 leans on the Event Registration Tool as its core measurement mechanism**, the same tool whose mandatory-use-for-reporting policy is unresolved and contested at the movement level. If that policy fight goes a different direction, Doelstelling 2's headline KPI methodology may need to change mid-plan.
- **The dropped diversity baseline** (§3, gap 1) is worth a direct check: deliberate simplification into the 2026 SWOT, or a metric that quietly didn't make the cut this cycle.
- **Your comment on whether contributors should be able to withhold content from AI training** (§3, gap 3) is still unresolved in the approved text — worth deciding whether that needs to be a year-one agenda item rather than staying implicit.
